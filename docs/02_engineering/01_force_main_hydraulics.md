# Force Main Hydraulics
The Force Main Hydraulics module is designed to analyze the hydraulics of a force main system, including the calculation of head loss, pump performance, and system efficiency. This document will be used to define force-main boundary conditions: suction/wet-well level, discharge HGL, pressure conditions, and elevation datum.  This module provides engineers with the tools necessary to evaluate and optimize the design of force mains in various applications.

The module includes features for calculating friction losses, determining the required pump head, and assessing the overall performance of the force main system. It is intended to be used in conjunction with other modules in the Hydraulic Analysis Suite to provide a comprehensive analysis of water and wastewater systems. The force main hydraulics module is built to support the design and evaluation of force main systems to assist engineers in the design of lift stations and pumping infrastructure.

## Force Main Boundary Conditions
The following boundary conditions are defined for the force main hydraulics module:
- Suction/wet-well level
- Discharge HGL
- Pressure conditions
- Elevation datum

### Suction/Wet-Well Level
The suction/wet-well level is the elevation of the water surface in the wet well or suction tank, which is the point where the force main system begins. This boundary condition is important because it affects the head loss and pump performance in the system. The suction/wet-well level can be measured using a water level gauge or a pressure transducer, and it is typically expressed in feet or meters above the elevation datum.

### Discharge HGL
The discharge HGL is the elevation of the water surface at the discharge point of the force main system, which is the point where the force main ends. This boundary condition is important because it affects the head loss and pump performance in the system. The discharge HGL can be measured using a water level gauge or a pressure transducer, and it is typically expressed in feet or meters above the elevation datum.

### Pressure Conditions
The pressure conditions in the force main system are defined by the pressure at the suction/wet-well level and the discharge HGL. These pressure conditions can be measured using pressure transducers or other pressure measurement devices, and they are typically expressed in pounds per square inch (psi) or kilopascals (kPa). The pressure conditions in the force main system can affect the head loss and pump performance in the system, and they are important to consider when designing and evaluating force main systems.

### Elevation Datum
The elevation datum is the reference point from which all elevations in the force main system are measured. It is typically chosen to be a known benchmark or a local mean sea level. The elevation datum is important because it provides a consistent reference for measuring the suction/wet-well level, discharge HGL, and other elevations in the system. All head calculations and hydraulic analyses are performed relative to this datum.

### HGL and Pressure Relationship
The relationship between the discharge HGL and pressure conditions in the force main system is important because it affects the head loss and pump performance in the system. The pressure at the discharge HGL is typically lower than the pressure at the suction/wet-well level due to the head loss in the system. The pressure difference between the suction/wet-well level and the discharge HGL is known as the head loss, and it is typically expressed in feet or meters of water column. The head loss can be calculated using various methods, such as the Hazen-Williams equation or the Darcy-Weisbach equation, and it is an important factor to consider when designing and evaluating force main systems.

### EGL
The energy grade line (EGL) represents the total energy in the force main system, including the pressure head, velocity head, and elevation head. The EGL is typically plotted above the hydraulic grade line (HGL) and provides a visual representation of the energy distribution in the system. Understanding the EGL is important for designing and evaluating force main systems because it helps identify potential issues such as cavitation, air entrainment, and energy losses.

The Force Main Hydraulics module provides engineers with the tools necessary to analyze and optimize the design of force main systems. By defining the suction/wet-well level, discharge HGL, pressure, and EGL, engineers can ensure that the force main system is efficient, reliable, and safe.
