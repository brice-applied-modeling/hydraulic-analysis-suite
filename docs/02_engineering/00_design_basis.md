# Design Basis

This document outlines the design basis for the project, including the key principles, constraints, and assumptions that guide the development process.  It defines the scope of the project, the intended use cases, and the engineering standards that must be adhered to.  The design basis serves as a reference for all stakeholders and ensures that the project meets its objectives while maintaining safety, reliability, and performance.

## Design Principles
This project is guided by the following design principles:
- Pipe Design: The project will adhere to established engineering standards for pipe design, including considerations for flow rates, pressure drops, and material selection.
- Pump System Design: The project will follow best practices for pump system design, including pump selection and sizing.
- Valve and Fitting Design: The project will consider the impact of valves and fittings on system performance, including pressure losses and flow distribution.
- Coefficients and Factors: The project will use appropriate coefficients and factors for calculations, including Hazen-Williams coefficients, friction factors, and other relevant parameters.
- Safety and Reliability: The project will prioritize safety and reliability in all design decisions, ensuring that the system operates within acceptable limits and mitigates potential risks.
- Real-World Applicability: The project will focus on practical applications and real-world scenarios, ensuring that the design basis reflects the needs of engineers and stakeholders in the field.
- Documentation and Communication: The project will maintain clear and concise documentation, facilitating effective communication among team members and stakeholders.
- Continuous Improvement: The project will incorporate feedback and lessons learned to continuously improve the design basis and overall project outcomes.

## Scope and Objectives
The scope of the project includes the development of a modular suite of reusable tools for water and wastewater engineering workflows. The objectives are to strengthen understanding of hydraulic engineering, develop professional software engineering skills, and create a foundation for future infrastructure analytics applications. 

## Intended Use Cases
The intended use cases for the project include:
- Designing and analyzing water and wastewater distribution systems
- Designing and evaluating pump stations and lift stations
- Performing hydraulic calculations for pipes, pumps, valves, and fittings
- Evaluating pump system performance and efficiency
- Assessing valve and fitting impacts on system performance
- Calculating coefficients and factors for hydraulic calculations
- Ensuring safety and reliability in engineering designs
- Facilitating effective communication among team members and stakeholders
- Supporting continuous improvement and learning within the engineering team
- Providing a foundation for future infrastructure analytics applications

## Limitations and Assumptions
The design basis is subject to the following limitations and assumptions:
- The project is focused on water and wastewater engineering applications and may not be applicable to other types of infrastructure systems.
- The tools developed within the project are designed for use in the United States and may not be applicable in other regions with different engineering standards and practices.
- The project assumes a certain level of familiarity with hydraulic engineering principles and software development best practices among the team members.
- The project does not address all possible scenarios and edge cases that may arise in real-world engineering applications.
- The project assumes that the tools developed will be used in conjunction with other engineering tools and software, and may not be standalone solutions.
- The project assumes that the tools developed will be used by qualified engineers and professionals with appropriate training and experience in hydraulic engineering and software development.
- The project assumes that the tools developed will be subject to ongoing maintenance and updates to address any issues or improvements identified during use.
- The project assumes that the tools developed will be used in accordance with applicable laws, regulations, and industry standards.
- The project assumes that the tools developed will be used in a manner consistent with the intended use cases and limitations outlined in this document.
- The project assumes that the tools developed will be used responsibly and ethically, with consideration for potential environmental and societal impacts.

## Fluid Mechanics and Hydraulic Principles
The project is based on fundamental principles of fluid mechanics and hydraulics, including the conservation of mass and momentum, as well as the principles of energy conservation and fluid flow through pipes and channels.

### Fluid Properties
The project assumes that the fluid being analyzed is incompressible and has a constant density. The fluid properties are assumed to be known and constant throughout the analysis. The project assumes that the fluid properties are within the range of typical values for water and wastewater applications. The following fluid properties are assumed for the purposes of this project:
- Density: 62.4 lb/ft³ (1000 kg/m³)
- Viscosity: 1.002 x 10^-3 Pa·s (1 cP)
- Temperature: 60°F (15.6°C)
- The water distribution system is assumed to be operating under normal conditions, with no significant changes in temperature or pressure that would affect the fluid properties.
- The wastewater collection system is assumed to be operating under normal conditions, with no significant changes in temperature or pressure that would affect the fluid properties and flow characteristics.
- The chemical composition of the water and wastewater is assumed to be within typical ranges for municipal systems, and any variations in chemical composition are assumed to have negligible effects on the fluid properties and flow characteristics.

### Hydraulic Principles
The project assumes that the hydraulic principles used in the analysis are consistent with the principles of fluid mechanics and hydraulics. The project assumes that the hydraulic principles used are appropriate for the intended use cases and limitations outlined in this document.

The following hydraulic principles are assumed for the purposes of this project:
- The flow of fluid through pipes and channels is assumed to be steady and incompressible.
- The flow of fluid through pipes and channels is assumed to be laminar or turbulent, depending on the Reynolds number and flow conditions.
- The head loss in pipes and channels is assumed to be proportional to the square of the flow rate, and is calculated using appropriate equations and coefficients.
- The effects of minor losses, such as those due to fittings, bends, and valves, are assumed to be negligible or are accounted for using appropriate correction factors.
- The friction factor used in the analysis is assumed to be appropriate for the flow conditions and pipe material, and is calculated using established equations and correlations.
- The effects of fluid compressibility and non-Newtonian behavior are assumed to be negligible or are accounted for using appropriate correction factors.

### Steady-State Flow Assumption
The project assumes that the flow of fluid through the system is steady-state, meaning that the flow rate and pressure do not change over time. This assumption allows for simplified analysis and modeling of the system, as well as the use of established equations and correlations for fluid flow and head loss calculations.

### Minor Losses and Correction Factors
The effects of minor losses, such as those due to fittings, bends, and valves, are assumed to be negligible or are accounted for using appropriate correction factors. These correction factors are based on established empirical data and are used to adjust the calculated head loss due to minor losses.

### Fluid Properties and Compressibility
The project assumes that the fluid properties, including density, viscosity, and compressibility, are constant and do not change over time. This assumption allows for simplified analysis and modeling of the system, as well as the use of established equations and correlations for fluid flow and head loss calculations. The effects of fluid compressibility and non-Newtonian behavior are assumed to be negligible or are accounted for using appropriate correction factors.
